## F8 — DDJ-REV1 dropped off the USB bus three times (2026-08-10)

During the PHI-171 session, the DDJ-REV1 disconnected from the machine
three times, unprompted and without being touched. Each time it
disappeared from CoreAudio and the MIDI device list together. Serato DJ
Pro remained running throughout.

- Three drops across roughly two hours, no timing pattern established.
- Each drop removed the device from both CoreAudio and CoreMIDI at once,
  pointing at the USB connection rather than either subsystem alone.
- Serato stayed at ~47% CPU at the third drop and did not crash.
- The device returned after a physical reconnect; unknown whether it
  would have returned on its own.

See PHI-181 for the investigation into cause.

## PHI-181 findings (append below as the investigation proceeds)

### Session — 2026-08-23

Ran `tools/ddj-rev1-monitor/monitor.py` against the real DDJ-REV1 across
four windows. Log: `tools/ddj-rev1-monitor/ddj-rev1-monitor.log`
(not committed — see `.gitignore`).

**1. Idle baseline** — 15:42–16:30 ET, ~48 min, 548 polls. Controller
connected, Serato not in active use. Zero drops.

**2. Active-use test** — 19:05–22:58 ET, ~3h 53min, thousands of polls.
Serato open, controller in normal use (jog wheels, faders, play/pause,
track loads). Result:

- Two unexplained drops early in this window, close together:
  - **19:14:34 → 19:14:55** (21s). Device left and returned on its own;
    no manual action reported at that time.
  - **19:15:32 → 19:19:24** (~3m 52s). Device left and returned. A manual
    unplug/replug was reported during this session, but the exact time
    was never pinned down — **this gap is inconclusive**, not confirmed
    as either a spontaneous drop or a fully-explained manual action.
- After that, the remaining ~3h 40min of active use ran clean — zero
  further drops. One later drop at 22:58:08–22:58:23 (19s) was a
  deliberately marked manual disconnect/reconnect, used to confirm the
  monitor itself catches a real physical unplug correctly. Confirmed
  working as expected.
- CPU load during the clean stretch stayed low (roughly 5–13 on the
  1-minute average), well under the ~47% Serato CPU noted at the original
  F8 drops.
- The kernel USB log watcher (`log stream` on
  `subsystem == "com.apple.iokit.usb"`) ran the whole session but never
  logged a single matching line, even during confirmed drops — it did
  not contribute useful data this run. Worth checking separately whether
  the predicate or `log stream` needs elevated permissions on this
  machine; the presence-polling channel carried the whole investigation.

**3. Port-swap test** — 22:59–23:31 ET, ~30.5 min, 352 polls. Same
controller, same cable, moved to a different USB port on the Mac
(confirmed via `ioreg`: device enumerated under a new USB node,
`DDJ-REV1@02100000` vs. the original `@01100000`). Zero drops.

**Conclusion so far:** could not reproduce a confirmed spontaneous drop
in this session. One ~4-minute gap (19:15:32–19:19:24) remains
unresolved and should not be counted as either a reproduction or a
non-event. Everything else — idle, ~3h40min of clean active use on the
original port, and 30.5 min clean on a different port — ran with zero
unexplained drops. Not yet tested: different cable, powered hub, battery
vs. mains power. No repair/replace decision is warranted yet given the
lack of a confirmed reproduction.

**Next steps:** if drops recur, get a precise timestamp for any manual
action immediately (don't reconstruct it afterward — this cost us
confidence in the 19:15 gap). Consider fixing the kernel log watcher
before the next session, since it produced no data this run. Remaining
variables to test one at a time: different cable, powered hub, battery
power.
